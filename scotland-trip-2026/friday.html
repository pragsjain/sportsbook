What to expect at each Day 1 stop

Royal Mile

Think of the Royal Mile as Edinburgh Old Town’s main historic street rather than one single attraction. It is lined with old buildings, narrow closes and wynds, cafés, pubs and lots of shops. Expect tartan and kilt stores, wool and cashmere, whisky shops, Scottish jewellery, local art and prints, souvenir shops and small independents. The point is to walk it slowly, browse, look into the side lanes and enjoy the atmosphere on the way toward the castle.

St Giles’ Cathedral

Yes, you can go inside. St Giles’ is Edinburgh’s historic High Kirk, founded in 1124 and closely connected with the Scottish Reformation and John Knox. Inside, look for the stained glass, Thistle Chapel, memorials, organ and layered medieval architecture. Current Friday visitor hours are 10:00–18:00. Allow around 20–30 minutes for a relaxed visit.

Victoria Street

This is the colourful curved street just below the castle and one of Edinburgh’s most photogenic areas. The shops are more boutique and quirky than on the Royal Mile: books, fantasy/Harry-Potter-style gifts, whisky, food specialists, vintage-style shops and unusual souvenirs. It is a short stop rather than a full shopping destination.

Grassmarket

Grassmarket is the historic square below the castle. The main appeal is the castle view, cobbled Old Town feel, cafés, pubs and independent shops. It is good for a relaxed pause after Victoria Street, especially if the parents want to sit down while others browse.

Calton Hill

Calton Hill is mainly for the panoramic skyline: Edinburgh Castle, Old Town, Princes Street, Arthur’s Seat and the Firth of Forth, plus the National Monument and other monuments on the summit. For a normal fit adult, the usual uphill walk takes about 10–15 minutes. For parents who would rather avoid the climb, a taxi can use the access road and drop you near Collective / City Observatory, leaving only a short amount of walking around the top. Do not worry about climbing Nelson Monument unless you specifically want to; that is a separate stair climb.

Best timing: aim to reach Calton Hill around 6:45–7:00 PM for sunset light on this date. Around 8 PM is still fine for city views, but sunset will already have passed.

Priority: Royal Mile + St Giles + Castle exterior + Victoria Street + Grassmarket. Optional: Mary King’s Close + Greyfriars. Calton Hill: keep it for sunset, preferably by taxi to the top for the parents. Castle interior: deliberately skipped.

👟 Walking plan — parent-friendly, updated

Decision locked in: you are not going inside Edinburgh Castle. You will only walk up to the exterior/esplanade, take in the views and continue. That removes the most tiring part of the castle visit.

Overall walking: if you do the full Old Town route on foot, expect roughly 2.5–3.5 km of walking across the afternoon, mostly broken into short sections. With a taxi for Calton Hill and a taxi/bus from Waverley to the apartment, your parents can keep the sightseeing walk closer to about 1.8–2.5 km.

Waverley → apartment / Milton Street: about 1.3–1.6 km if walked, roughly 20–25 min and partly uphill. Recommendation: take a taxi/bus with luggage rather than count this as sightseeing walking.
Apartment → Royal Mile / Canongate: roughly 500–700 m, about 8–12 min. Gentle city walking.
Royal Mile browsing → St Giles’: roughly 700–900 m depending where you join the Royal Mile, about 12–18 min, mostly gradual uphill with cobbles. Because you will stop at shops and closes, this will feel easier than one continuous walk.
St Giles’ Cathedral: only a few minutes of walking inside. Allow 20–30 min to look around. Seating is available, so this is also a useful rest stop.
St Giles’ → Mary King’s Close: almost next door — roughly 50–100 m, about 1–2 min. If you book the tour, most of the effort is standing/walking inside rather than getting there.
St Giles / Mary King’s Close → Edinburgh Castle exterior: about 500–600 m, roughly 8–12 min. This is the steepest continuous uphill section of the Old Town route, but you are stopping at the esplanade only — no interior castle climb.
Castle esplanade: roughly 200–300 m of easy wandering for photos and views, around 10–15 min. No ticket needed for the exterior/esplanade area.
Castle → Victoria Street: around 300–400 m, about 5–7 min, mostly downhill. Victoria Street itself is short; browsing the full street adds roughly another 200–300 m.
Victoria Street → Grassmarket: around 200–300 m, roughly 4–6 min downhill. Grassmarket is a good place for parents to sit for coffee while others browse.
Grassmarket → Greyfriars Kirkyard: roughly 400–500 m, about 7–10 min. Greyfriars itself may add another 300–500 m depending how much of the kirkyard you explore. Optional if parents are tired.
Calton Hill: from the lower city approach, a normal fit adult takes about 10–15 min uphill. For your parents, use the easier plan: taxi to Collective / City Observatory near the top. Then expect only about 300–600 m of relatively easy walking around the summit viewpoints. Skip Nelson Monument stairs.

Best parent version: taxi/bus Waverley → apartment → walk Royal Mile → St Giles → Castle exterior → Victoria Street → Grassmarket → optional Greyfriars → taxi to Calton Hill → taxi back/down.

If energy drops: skip Greyfriars first. Keep Royal Mile, Castle exterior, Victoria Street and Grassmarket. Calton Hill can still work because you can taxi almost to the top.

Arrival / Edinburgh

Edinburgh has been on my list for so long and somehow the first thing I noticed is that this city is basically built on hills, stairs and dramatic stone buildings 😂

First impression of Edinburgh: dark stone, steep streets, tiny lanes, bagpipes somewhere in the background… very subtle, Scotland.

One afternoon in Edinburgh and I already understand why people say this city feels like a movie set. Every second street looks suspiciously cinematic.

Edinburgh is basically two personalities living together: medieval Old Town on one side, elegant Georgian New Town on the other. Very “I contain multitudes.”

Waverley

Edinburgh Waverley might be one of the most dramatic train arrivals ever. You literally step out between Old Town and New Town.

Small useful tip: the black cabs outside Waverley were faster for us than waiting for an Uber. Straight into the cab, straight to the apartment.

Royal Mile

The Royal Mile is basically the historic spine of Edinburgh Old Town — castle at one end, Holyrood Palace at the other. Everything in between is old stone buildings, closes, pubs, shops and a lot of history.

Walking the Royal Mile is less about one attraction and more about constantly stopping because some random alley looks 500 years old.

These tiny side lanes are called closes and wynds. Basically Edinburgh’s version of “just one more lane, I promise.”

Scottish shopping

Scottish shopping is very specific and I respect the commitment: tartan, wool, kilts, whisky… and more tartan.

Tartan isn’t simply one Scottish check pattern. Different tartans became associated over time with clans, regions and institutions — so there is a lot more history behind those colourful patterns than I realised.

The wool and cashmere game here is strong. Scotland really said: you will be cold, but you will look expensive.

St Giles

St Giles’ was one of those places I almost treated as a quick stop… and then went inside and realised there is actually so much history here.

That crown-shaped tower on St Giles’ is called the Crown Steeple and it is one of the most recognisable parts of Edinburgh’s skyline.

St Giles’ is closely tied to the Scottish Reformation and John Knox. So this wasn’t just a church — it sat right in the middle of major religious and political change in Scotland.

The Thistle Chapel inside St Giles’ is tiny compared with the main church, but the wood carving is insanely detailed. One of those corners you could easily miss if you rush through.

Mary King’s Close

One of the wildest things about Edinburgh: there are old streets and rooms hidden underneath the Royal Mile.

Mary King’s Close is a preserved piece of old Edinburgh below the modern city — cramped homes, trades, poor sanitation and plague-era life. History suddenly feels much more human when it is about how ordinary families actually lived.

Edinburgh Castle

We skipped the inside of Edinburgh Castle today and, with only one afternoon, I’m glad we did. The exterior and esplanade still give you the full dramatic castle moment.

Edinburgh Castle sits on ancient volcanic rock. Standing up here, you immediately understand why this was such a powerful defensive position — you can see the city spreading out below you.

Victoria Street

Victoria Street is the colourful curved street everyone photographs in Edinburgh… and yes, it is very cute in real life.

This is the street people associate with Diagon Alley vibes — curved cobbled street, colourful shops and old buildings. It wasn’t a Harry Potter filming location, but visually I completely get the comparison.

Victoria Street is tiny, so don’t plan half a day here. Come, walk, browse, take photos, buy something unnecessary, continue 😂

Grassmarket

Grassmarket looks like a cute café square now, but it has a much darker past — it was once a site of public executions.

One of my favourite views here is looking back up at Edinburgh Castle looming over the rooftops. Very subtle castle placement.

Apparently people used to come here for executions. I came for a coffee and a castle photo. Progress.

Greyfriars

Greyfriars Kirkyard somehow manages to feel peaceful, creepy and beautiful at the same time. It is one of Edinburgh’s historic graveyards and is packed with old stones and stories.

The famous Greyfriars Bobby story is about a little Skye terrier remembered for staying close to his owner’s grave for years. Small dog, massive Edinburgh celebrity.

Calton Hill

Calton Hill is one of the easiest ways to get a full Edinburgh skyline view without doing a serious hike. My kind of mountain.

From up here you can see the Castle, Old Town, Princes Street, Arthur’s Seat, Leith and the Firth of Forth. Basically the whole city doing a group photo.

That unfinished Greek-looking structure is the National Monument. It was inspired by the Parthenon and begun as a memorial to Scots who died in the Napoleonic Wars… then the money ran out. Relatable.

Calton Hill at sunset is one of those places where you stop taking photos for two minutes and just stare. Then immediately start taking photos again.

Extra

Edinburgh is the kind of city where every street seems to have either a ghost story, an execution story, a royal story — or all three.

The city is beautiful, but your calves will know about it tomorrow.

I came here thinking the castle would be the main character. Honestly, Old Town itself is the main character.

Scotland so far: 10/10 scenery, 10/10 history, 4/10 flat roads.

Current travel strategy: history for the brain, coffee for the legs, sunset for Instagram.
