👟 Walking plan — parent-friendly

Overall: this is the only day where I would deliberately plan two versions. The full hiking version can easily exceed 6 km; the parent-friendly sightseeing version can stay around 1–2 km in short sections.

Old Man of Storr: the full out-and-back trail is about 3.8 km and is the toughest walk of the trip. Let the fitter group do the hike if conditions are good. Parents can walk only the first 10–20 minutes, turn back when they want, and wait at/near the car park rather than feeling they must complete it.

Lealt Falls + Kilt Rock: excellent parent stops — short walks from parking, high scenic payoff.

Quiraing: treat the roadside viewpoint as the main stop for the parents. The younger/fitter group can do a short out-and-back while parents enjoy the view without committing to a trail.

Fairy Glen: optional for the parents because the ground is uneven and the small hills add up. If energy is good, do a short 20–30 minute wander; otherwise skip it.

Best strategy: do not make Storr + Quiraing hikes compulsory for everyone. Split for 45–90 minutes, then regroup at the car. Skip first if tired: Fairy Glen, then any extra west-coast extension such as Neist Point.

Skye morning

Full Skye day. The plan looks like a list of attractions, but honestly the landscape between them is half the experience.

Today’s rule: if the clouds give us a view, we take it. Skye weather is basically the trip manager.

Old Man of Storr

The Old Man of Storr is part of the Trotternish landscape, shaped by a huge ancient landslip. Geology, but make it cinematic.

Storr from the road already looks dramatic. Walking closer just keeps changing the scale.

Skye hiking tip: visibility matters. There is no prize for climbing into a cloud just because it was on the itinerary.

Lealt + Kilt Rock

Lealt Falls: short stop, big payoff. Exactly the kind of place I love adding between longer walks.

Kilt Rock gets its name from the vertical rock columns and layered appearance that resemble the pleats of a kilt.

Mealt Falls + Kilt Rock is basically a roadside viewpoint behaving like a full attraction.

Quiraing

The Quiraing is part of the enormous Trotternish landslip — twisted ridges, cliffs and green plateaus that look slightly unreal even when you are standing inside them.

If I had to choose one landscape stop on north Skye to simply stand and stare at, Quiraing would be very hard to beat.

Quiraing is the kind of place where wide-angle still isn’t wide enough.

Fairy Glen

Fairy Glen feels like somebody made a miniature version of Skye — little green hills, tiny lochans and strange shapes everywhere.

No, I did not see fairies. Yes, I still understand the name.

Fairy Glen is less about one big viewpoint and more about wandering around wondering why the landscape looks so tiny and strange.

Portree

Portree is the colourful harbour pause after a day of very dramatic Skye landscapes.

Skye today: rocks older than my comprehension, weather changing every ten minutes, camera roll completely out of control.

Weather plan

This is the most weather-sensitive day. Low cloud and wind matter more than temperature. Typical daytime 12–15°C. Reorder Storr and Quiraing around visibility; waterproof footwear strongly recommended.
