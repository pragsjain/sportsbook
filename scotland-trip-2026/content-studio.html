Strategy: problems before places

Every episode starts with a question a traveller actually has. Then we answer it using firsthand footage, real routes, decisions, mistakes, short walks, and honest reviews. We test three repeatable formats instead of guessing what will go viral.

1 · Things nobody tells you

Curiosity-led, personality-rich voiceover reels. Measure non-follower reach, shares and follows.

2 · Exact itinerary

Mobile-readable maps and day-wise RGF. Measure saves, shares and guide clicks.

3 · Honest review

Rental car, accommodation, vegetarian meals and conditional real budget. Measure questions, saves and follows.

Distinctive perspective: London-based Indian travellers exploring Scotland with parents, vegetarian meals, limited hiking and genuine road-trip logistics. Don't box every post into that identity: lead with the broad travel problem when appropriate.

Publishing: Hero reel → master route → early mistakes reel (proven format) → mix discovery and saveable guides based on audience interest, not strict chronology → food, stay and conditional budget follow-ups. Each post needs a standalone hook; don't lead with “Episode 4.”

Accuracy: This is an editorial plan. The original day guides are partly pre-trip plans. Actual stops, costs and times must be confirmed from photos, videos or records. The Day 5 return used A86/A9 via Pitlochry, not the outdated Loch Ness/Inverness plan. No audience performance or viral scores are invented.

Content formula: INSPIRE → HELP → PROVE → CONVERT. Start with the traveller's problem, show your original footage and a firsthand insight, then offer one reason to save, share or follow.

Reel pattern: 0–2 seconds hook; 2–5 seconds proof you were there; 5–20 seconds story/useful advice; 20–30 seconds payoff; one natural CTA. Adjust to the actual story rather than forcing exactly 30 seconds.

Visual identity: main cover hook 3–7 words; small MG/Scotland label; consistent typography and subtle colour accents, never branding over the destination. Use original footage. Include your face or candid reactions where they genuinely improve the story.

Publishing test: Strategy suggests four reels plus one carousel per week across the whole account, not five Scotland posts every week. Mix with Venice, Greece and London/UK; reassess after the first 20 posts. This Scotland inventory is a flexible library, not a rigid publishing calendar.

Conversion: Optimise profile name/bio for Europe/UK planning and pin an introduction, useful guide and personality post. Avoid making “Episode 1” the main headline.

What makes a post worth saving?

For each day carousel: accurate 4:5 map + compact RGF + must-do stops + skip-if-late + walking difficulty + food/rest + firsthand verdict. For voiceovers: a problem in the first 2 seconds, one personal insight, and a satisfying visual payoff.

RGF: TIME Place (⭐ priority | km from previous | Stop duration | Walk | Rest/food | ⏰ timing constraints).

Workflow: Idea → Actual trip verified → Script → Media selected → Editing → Ready → Posted → Insights logged.

Stories: Use 3–6 purposeful stories on active days, such as route polls, stay comparisons and ‘guess the cost’, to learn what followers want next.

Learning loop: Compare hook retention, reach to non-followers, saves, shares, profile visits and follows after posting. Refine future formats based on results; no made-up 3× or 4× claims.

Pinned profile posts: “Start here” intro + strongest proven useful guide (possibly the Scotland route) + a personality/adventure reel. Choose based on conversion, not just views.
