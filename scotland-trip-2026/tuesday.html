👟 Walking plan — parent-friendly

Overall: keep Tuesday deliberately light — roughly 1–2 km easy walking at most. The priority is the long drive and the fixed 19:20 train.

Fort Augustus: best parent stop of the day. The canal/lock area is relatively flat and easy to enjoy with a short 20–30 minute wander.

Urquhart Castle: the site has steep ramps and some stairs. For an easier visit, use the visitor centre/viewpoint and do not descend through the full ruins. The site has wheelchairs and mobility scooters on a first-come basis if needed.

Inverness: keep lunch close to parking. No city walking loop.

Flexible rule: if anyone is tired, skip Urquhart ruins entirely and keep just Fort Augustus + lunch. Saving energy and protecting the Edinburgh arrival buffer matters more today.

Skip first if behind schedule: Urquhart interior, then Inverness sightseeing. Do not sacrifice the car-return/train buffer.

Leaving Skye

Last Scotland road-trip day and the dangerous temptation is trying to squeeze in 'just one more stop' before a fixed evening train.

Leaving Skye early because today’s most important attraction is technically… making the train back to London.

Fort Augustus

Fort Augustus sits at the southern end of Loch Ness where the Caledonian Canal meets the loch — an easy place to see the locks without committing to a long stop.

Coffee, canal locks and Loch Ness. Very efficient final-day sightseeing.

Loch Ness + Urquhart

Loch Ness is huge enough that driving beside it still doesn’t quite prepare you for the scale.

Urquhart Castle has one of the classic Loch Ness settings — ruined stone walls with the loch stretching out behind them.

Final-day rule: viewpoint if we are late, ruins if we have buffer. Travel plans work better when every stop has a shorter version.

Inverness

Inverness: capital of the Highlands and today, very honestly, our lunch-and-fuel checkpoint before the long drive south.

Not every city needs a three-hour walking tour. Sometimes the smart travel version is food, fuel, stretch, continue.

A9 + going home

The A9 south is the less glamorous part of the itinerary but the scenery still refuses to become boring.

Five days, one train north, one Highland road trip, one island and far too many photos later — heading back to London.

Scotland trip takeaway: leave space in the itinerary. The unplanned viewpoints were often as memorable as the famous stops.
