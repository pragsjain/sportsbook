The trip at a glance

Travel north by train, spend the first afternoon exploring Edinburgh on foot, then hire a car for the Highlands and Skye. The route uses three overnight areas: Edinburgh, Caol near Fort William, and Kyleakin on Skye.

Distance and driving figures are route-planning estimates. Allow extra time for slower Highland roads, stops and transfers.

How the route fits together

Edinburgh gets a car-free first day. The next two days move west through Glencoe and Glenfinnan to Skye. A full day is set aside for the island before the long return via Loch Ness.

Keep the long drives simple. Choose a few worthwhile stops and protect the time needed to reach your overnight area comfortably.

Day 1 · Friday 18 September

London to Edinburgh

🚆 Train day · no car needed

King’s Cross → Edinburgh Waverley → Old Town on foot

Suggested flow

  • Take a train north that arrives around lunchtime, leaving time to drop bags and refresh.
  • Walk the Royal Mile and visit St Giles’ Cathedral if it is open.
  • Continue to Edinburgh Castle’s exterior and esplanade, then walk down to Victoria Street and Grassmarket.
  • Finish at Calton Hill or the New Town depending on energy and daylight.

Keep the afternoon relaxed

We left the castle interior out of this short first day to keep the walking route manageable.

Prioritise: Royal Mile, St Giles’, castle exterior, Victoria Street and Grassmarket.
Optional: Mary King’s Close, Greyfriars and Calton Hill.

Day 2 · Saturday 19 September

Edinburgh to Glencoe via Rannoch Moor

🚗 ~155 mi · ~4¾ hrs driving plus stops

Edinburgh → Callander → Tyndrum → Rannoch Moor / Loch Tulla → Glen Etive → Three Sisters → Caol

Our route

  • Pick up the car in Edinburgh and head west. A later start meant we skipped a castle visit in Stirling.
  • Stop in Callander for lunch and a proper rest; use Tyndrum for fuel, coffee or toilets if needed.
  • Pause at one or two safe viewpoints around Rannoch Moor and Loch Tulla.
  • Drive a short section of Glen Etive for the mountain scenery, then turn back rather than trying to reach the loch.
  • Stop at the Three Sisters viewpoint. Add a short Glencoe Lochan walk only if time and energy allow, then continue to Caol.

What to protect

Keep: Rannoch Moor and the Three Sisters. They give this drive its Highland character.

Shorten first: Stirling sightseeing, Tyndrum and Glen Etive. Set a turn-back point on the Glen Etive road.

Lost Valley is a proper mountain hike, not a quick viewpoint add-on.

Day 3 · Sunday 20 September

Glenfinnan to the Isle of Skye

🚗 ~129 mi · ~4 hrs driving plus stops

Caol / Fort William → Glenfinnan → Commando Memorial → Loch Garry → Eilean Donan → Kyleakin

Our route

  • Leave Caol in the morning for Glenfinnan. Use marked parking and allow time to walk to a viaduct viewpoint.
  • If seeing the Jacobite steam train is part of your plan, check the operator’s current timetable and arrive with a buffer; crossing times vary.
  • Continue to the Commando Memorial, then stop briefly at the Loch Garry viewpoint.
  • Spend time at Eilean Donan Castle’s causeway and exterior. Go inside only if the day is comfortably on schedule.
  • Cross the Skye Bridge and settle in around Kyleakin.

Make the timing work

Prioritise: Glenfinnan and Eilean Donan’s exterior setting.

Short stops: Commando Memorial and Loch Garry.

If delayed, skip the castle interior and keep the evening simple after reaching Skye.

Day 4 · Monday 21 September

A full day on the Isle of Skye

🚗 ~130 mi · ~3¾ hrs driving before stops

Kyleakin → Old Man of Storr → Lealt Falls → Kilt Rock & Mealt Falls → Quiraing → Fairy Glen → Portree → Kyleakin

Our route

  • Head north early enough to give the Old Man of Storr and Quiraing room in the day.
  • Choose the Storr walk based on time, footing and energy; the lower viewpoint is a shorter alternative.
  • Use Lealt Falls and Kilt Rock as compact stops along the coast.
  • At the Quiraing, enjoy the viewpoint and add only a short out-and-back walk if conditions are suitable.
  • Keep Fairy Glen and Portree flexible. Drop one if the day is running long.

Choose your pace

Gentler day: short viewpoints, no required full hikes, and an early return.

More walking: focus on one longer hike, then shorten optional stops.

Allow extra time for narrow roads and photo stops. Avoid driving the final stretch tired.

Day 5 · Tuesday 22 September

Skye to Loch Ness and Edinburgh

🚗 ~250 mi · ~6 hrs driving plus stops

Kyleakin → Fort Augustus → Urquhart Castle / Loch Ness → Inverness → A9 → Edinburgh

Our route

  • Leave Kyleakin early for the long drive back to Edinburgh.
  • Stop at Fort Augustus for a short walk by the Caledonian Canal locks.
  • Choose a brief Urquhart Castle viewpoint or visit only if the road schedule allows.
  • Use Inverness for lunch, fuel and a stretch before heading south on the A9.
  • Return the car and reach Waverley with a generous buffer before your train south.

Keep the return calm

This is the day to resist extra detours. Protect the car return, fuel stop, city transfer and train connection.

If behind: shorten Urquhart first, keep Inverness practical, and protect the Edinburgh transfer buffer.

Practical notes

  • Allow extra time for single-track roads, passing places, livestock, traffic and photo stops.
  • Use marked parking and passing places correctly; never stop on the road for photos.
  • Download an offline map and plan fuel stops outside the larger towns.
  • Check current attraction opening times, road conditions, access and seasonal closures before travelling.
  • For Glenfinnan, check the Jacobite operator’s timetable directly if you want to plan around a train crossing.
Route estimates are a starting point. Recheck drive times, opening information and access rules for your own travel dates.
