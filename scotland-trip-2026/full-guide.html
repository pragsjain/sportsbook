Scotland in five days: Edinburgh, Highlands & Skye

A first-hand route from London to Edinburgh, through Glencoe and Glenfinnan, around the Isle of Skye, then back via Loch Ness. Built around realistic drive days, flexible stops and time to enjoy the scenery.

The trip at a glance

Four nights across Edinburgh, Caol near Fort William, and Kyleakin on Skye. We travelled north by train, hired a car in Edinburgh, and used the island stay to keep a full Skye day in the middle of the route.

What worked for this route

We kept the first day car-free, gave Skye a full day, and used shorter viewpoints between the big drives. The longest return day was planned around getting back to Edinburgh in time for the evening train.

Keep one thing flexible each day. In the Highlands, weather, road speed and photo stops can change the schedule quickly.

Day 1 · Friday 18 September

London to Edinburgh

🚆 Train day · No car needed

King’s Cross → Edinburgh Waverley → Old Town on foot

Our day

  • Take the train north and arrive around lunchtime; leave time to drop bags and refresh.
  • Walk the Royal Mile and step inside St Giles’ Cathedral if it is open.
  • Continue to the Castle Esplanade for the exterior views, then Victoria Street and Grassmarket.
  • Finish at Calton Hill or the New Town depending on energy, weather and sunset timing.

Good to know

We kept the castle interior out of this short afternoon so the route stayed manageable.

Prioritise: Royal Mile, St Giles’, castle exterior, Victoria Street and Grassmarket.
Optional: Mary King’s Close, Greyfriars and Calton Hill.

Day 2 · Saturday 19 September

Edinburgh to Glencoe via Rannoch Moor

🚗 ~155 mi · ~4¾ hrs driving plus stops

Edinburgh → Callander → Tyndrum → Rannoch Moor / Loch Tulla → Glen Etive → Three Sisters → Caol

Our day

  • Pick up the hire car in Edinburgh and head west. A later start meant we treated Stirling as a pass-through rather than a castle visit.
  • Stop in Callander for a proper lunch and rest break; use Tyndrum for fuel, coffee or toilets if needed.
  • Pause at one or two safe viewpoints around Rannoch Moor and Loch Tulla.
  • Drive a short section of Glen Etive for the mountain scenery, then turn back rather than trying to reach the loch.
  • Stop at the Three Sisters viewpoint. Add a short Glencoe Lochan walk only if weather, daylight and energy allow; continue to Caol for the night.

What we would protect

Keep: Rannoch Moor and the Three Sisters. They give this drive its Highland character.

Shorten first: Stirling sightseeing, Tyndrum and Glen Etive. The Glen Etive road is a scenic out-and-back, so set a turn-back point.

The Lost Valley is a proper mountain hike, not a quick viewpoint add-on.

Day 3 · Sunday 20 September

Glenfinnan to the Isle of Skye

🚗 ~129 mi · ~4 hrs driving plus stops

Caol / Fort William → Glenfinnan → Commando Memorial → Loch Garry → Eilean Donan → Kyleakin

Our day

  • Leave Caol in the morning for Glenfinnan. Use marked parking and allow time to walk to the viaduct viewpoints.
  • If seeing the Jacobite steam train is part of your plan, check the operator’s current timetable and arrive with a buffer; crossing times can vary.
  • Continue east to the Commando Memorial, then north for a short stop at Loch Garry viewpoint.
  • Spend time at Eilean Donan Castle’s causeway and exterior. Go inside only if the day is comfortably on schedule.
  • Cross the Skye Bridge and settle in around Kyleakin.

Make the timing work

Must-see: Glenfinnan and Eilean Donan’s exterior setting.

Short stops: Commando Memorial and Loch Garry.

If delayed: skip the castle interior and keep the evening simple after reaching Skye.

Day 4 · Monday 21 September

A full day on the Isle of Skye

🚗 ~130 mi · ~3¾ hrs driving before stops

Kyleakin → Old Man of Storr → Lealt Falls → Kilt Rock & Mealt Falls → Quiraing → Fairy Glen → Portree → Kyleakin

Our day

  • Head north early enough to give the Old Man of Storr and Quiraing room in the day.
  • Choose the Storr walk based on visibility, wind, footing and energy; the lower viewpoint is a shorter alternative.
  • Use Lealt Falls and Kilt Rock as compact stops along the coast.
  • At the Quiraing, enjoy the viewpoint and add only a short out-and-back walk if conditions are good.
  • Keep Fairy Glen and Portree flexible. Drop one if the day is running long or the weather changes.

Choose your pace

Gentler day: short viewpoints, no required full hikes, and an early return.

More walking: focus on one longer hike, then shorten optional stops.

The route is exposed to fast-changing weather. Keep the order flexible and avoid driving the final stretch tired or in poor visibility.

Day 5 · Tuesday 22 September

Skye to Loch Ness and Edinburgh

🚗 ~250 mi · ~6 hrs driving plus stops

Kyleakin → Fort Augustus → Urquhart Castle / Loch Ness → Inverness → A9 → Edinburgh

Our day

  • Leave Kyleakin early for the long drive back to Edinburgh.
  • Stop at Fort Augustus for a short walk by the Caledonian Canal locks.
  • Choose a brief Urquhart Castle viewpoint or visit only if the road schedule allows.
  • Use Inverness for lunch, fuel and a stretch before heading south on the A9.
  • Return the car and reach Waverley with a generous buffer before your train south.

Keep the return calm

This is the day to resist extra detours. Protect the car return, fuel stop, city transfer and train connection.

If behind: shorten Urquhart first, keep Inverness practical, and do not eat into the Edinburgh buffer.

Before using this route

  • Check current road conditions and weather each morning, especially on Skye and around Glencoe.
  • Recheck attraction opening times, parking rules, access and any seasonal closures.
  • Check the Jacobite train timetable directly with the operator if you want to plan around a crossing.
  • Allow extra time for single-track roads, passing places, livestock, traffic and photography stops.
  • Download offline maps and keep fuel stops in mind outside the larger towns.
Drone note: our public guide does not mark any location as automatically suitable for flying. Check current CAA airspace, local signs, landowner guidance, people, wildlife and weather at the exact take-off point.
